A validated harm direction that detects but does not correct

We built a harm-vs-safe direction for LLaDA-8B under DIJA prompt-injection and put it through the full gate sequence. It cleanly reads LLaDA's harm axis — and, tested causally in both the response it writes and the scaffold that forces the harm, it corrects nothing. A third causal confirmation of the paper's detection-not-correction thesis.

02The detector is real — Gate E

Gate E asked the question the earlier gates couldn't: does a direction fit on DeepSeek-authored harm/safe pairs point along LLaDA's own harm axis? We built a valid target — 36 matched-length LLaDA-authored harm/safe pairs (Jaccard 0.21) — and measured directional alignment.

Transfer AUC is the wrong metric here: the LLaDA harm and safe activation clusters sit so far apart that a random direction separates them at AUC 0.92–0.98, and norm alone reaches 0.97. So AUC saturates and says nothing. Directional alignment cuts through it — and the DeepSeek-built direction shares roughly half its cosine with LLaDA's own harm axis, an order of magnitude above chance. As a detector, it transfers.

The very dose that pulls harm down also truncates and collapses the benign injected output — the intervention can't tell the two apart. And the steer-early / remask-late variant (SR) tracked Arm S almost exactly — same harm curve, same C damage, same degeneracy — so the remask recipe bought no added specificity. A harm drop that a random push half-reproduces, that tracks a rising breakage rate, and that damages benign output identically is scaffold breakage, not a corrective lever.

05Why detection didn't become correction

Under DIJA, the harmful content is carried by the injected scaffold — the attacker's template with blanks — not by the model's free response. That single geometry explains both causal tests. Steer the response positions (Phase 3) and the scaffold keeps forcing the completion; a direction that reads harm downstream can't overpower the prompt dictating it, so wording shifts and substance doesn't. Steer the scaffold positions (Phase 3.1) and you finally perturb where the harm is forced — but the push is nonspecific: it disrupts everything the scaffold generates, harmful and benign alike, which is exactly why a random direction reproduces most of the drop, degeneracy tracks it, and the benign-injected control breaks identically. There is no selective corrective lever — only a detector reading an axis, and a blunt push that breaks generation before it induces refusal. Seen from a third angle, it's the same mechanism as the ~70% surface-form result and the re-masking falsification: harm lives in the template, so neither response-space nudges nor scaffold-space nudges convert reading harm into removing it.

The bottom line for the thesis. A direction can be a genuine, cross-model harm detector and still be causally inert as a corrector — in the region we generate and in the region that carries the attack. Three causal tests now separate reading from removing, and all three land on the detection-not-correction side.

06The one honest open question — not run

Both causal tests steered a DeepSeek-built direction that transfers to LLaDA only partially (cos ≈ 0.5). One test could still move the verdict; nothing is launched, the decision is the meeting's.

Steer with a truly LLaDA-native direction. Build vharm from the Gate E matched LLaDA harm/safe pairs — not the DeepSeek pairs — and steer with that. It isolates the one confound the current tests can't rule out: whether the partial ~0.5 transfer is the bottleneck, or whether harm-under-DIJA simply has no selective steering lever at all. Everything else — scaffold region, remask, multi-layer — has now been tried and lands on breakage or null; this is the single remaining test that could change the conclusion.
